Choose question wording after the scientific goal and reference solution. Preserve the natural
answer form and every locked deliverable; impose no answer-length or quantitative-only rule.
If the goal is a proof, stating the proposition to prove is legitimate; revealing its hidden
argument is not. A formula-only gold still cannot silently become a proof task during wording.
Do not lower the difficulty in the wording. The question states exactly what the paper starts from:
primitive objects and definitions, model laws, given data, quantifiers, conventions that change the
answer, and the requested final output. Do NOT add helper definitions, intermediate results, ansatzes,
effective quantities, decompositions, normalizations chosen by the proof, named theorems, suggested
methods or "hint" sentences, even if they would make the question shorter or look clearer.
If you are unsure whether a solver can recover a hidden step, keep it hidden as long as the paper
derives it from the retained premises; never add a hint for safety. Do not simplify the model,
reduce parameters or replace general objects by special cases beyond the locked target.
Ambiguity is not difficulty: conventions, units, normalizations and branch labels that change the
answer must still be defined in the question.
