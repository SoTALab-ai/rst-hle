INITIAL QUESTION TYPES (optional references, not mandatory quotas or a post-evaluation hardening loop):
1. inverse_identification: from specified outcome features recover ALL admissible parameters or
   structures, with an explicit equivalence convention when identification is not unique.
2. branch_discovery: use primitive constraints to establish which algebraic/physical branches are
   admissible, then compute the single target. Include degenerate and excluded branches in the proof.
3. parameter_region: derive necessary AND sufficient conditions for the complete validity region,
   including equality, singular, disconnected and empty regions. A grid scan or local example is not proof.
4. theorem_applicability: infer whether a result can be applied from primitive data, verify each
   hypothesis and handle exceptions. Keep object definitions even when the theorem's name is hidden.
Record the chosen construction_type and bridge_type when applicable; leave them null if none fits.
Do not force these types onto an already locked pointwise target or add outputs absent from its gold.

DEPENDENCY TRACEBACK FOR MASKING (inspired by AlphaGeometry, not a claim of formal verification):
After completing the proof, trace backwards from the actual target through numbered derivation entries.
List the primitive objects, definitions and independent premises needed to define the task. Separately
identify derived helper terms used by the proof but unnecessary to define those primitive objects:
an invariant, a spectral/change-of-representation bridge, an auxiliary object, a symmetry,
a conservation relation or a useful decomposition. Select a substantial necessary bridge and map it
to mask.proof_step_indices. Keep its construction and justification in the private full proof;
omit its explicit form, recipe and equivalent hints from the proposed solver-visible inputs.
In mask.recoverability describe the backward dependency path with proof-entry indices and explain
how retained primitive data permit reconstructing the helper. In retained_information keep sufficient
definitions, model laws, domains and conventions. Derived branch/range conditions may be unknowns
only when they follow from these data or are explicitly requested outputs. Never delete an independent
axiom or the definition of a physical model to simulate difficulty. Try a genuine shortcut using ONLY
the retained inputs. An auxiliary term redundant to the target is decoration, not a valid bridge.
These are construction hypotheses; the separate actual-question audit and real solver trials decide.

SHORTEST CORRECT ROUTE CHALLENGE:
Before expanding the proof, find the best valid route from the proposed retained inputs to the
literal requested result. In difficulty.baseline_shortcut describe this CORRECT route, its actual
premises and the burden it avoids or still has. A counterexample to dropping a term, ignoring a
branch, assuming commutation or using a wrong sign does not refute the best correct route.
Use shortcut_counterexample only for a real admissible limitation of that route; if none is known,
say so explicitly rather than inventing a witness. A valid short route is not defeated by making
the reference proof longer. Explain the comparison in mask.hardest_reason and inverse_construction.
Separate discovery of a useful helper/ansatz from verification after that helper is already supplied.
Count substantive inferences, not bookkeeping entries, output checklists or named skills. The most
difficult step in the available proof need not be sufficiently difficult for this project. If the
fixed target is routine, expose the short route honestly for depth review under the configured policy; do not
replace the locked target, pad the proof or add requests absent from its gold. Standard background
results may legitimately be used by a solver. A private proof does not require its reproduction
when the question and grading contract ask only for a final result, regardless of its length.
